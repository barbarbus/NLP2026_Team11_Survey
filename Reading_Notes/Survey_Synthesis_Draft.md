# Survey Synthesis Draft: Evaluating Hallucinations in LLMs

**Status.** This is an English argument draft for the ACL-format survey, grounded in the [comparative matrices](Comparison_Matrices.md) and [numerical evidence ledger](Numerical_Evidence_Ledger.md). Paper IDs are working citation keys, to be replaced by verified BibTeX keys during typesetting. The draft does not present results from a new model experiment.

## RQ1: What is being measured?

“Hallucination” is not one operational target. In source-grounded generation, a statement can be unsupported by the supplied document while remaining true in the world. Conversely, a response can repeat an incorrect source faithfully. The distinction is explicit in studies of summarization and knowledge-grounded question answering (C11; I041). Open-domain factuality benchmarks such as TruthfulQA (C05) instead test whether responses reproduce common false beliefs. These targets should be reported separately. A survey that combines them under one detection accuracy would obscure the reference against which an error was judged.

The evaluation unit also changes the interpretation of a score. FActScore (C07) decomposes a long response into atomic facts and reports source-relative factual precision. Its mean is conditional on answered prompts; without abstention rate and the number of facts per response, a higher score need not imply more useful information. RAGTruth (C12) provides character-level hallucination spans as well as answer-level labels; its reported answer-level and span-level F1 values measure different capabilities. In FELM (C13), a segment and an atomic claim are likewise different units, and the rule that all constituent claims must be correct can create a length-dependent segment penalty. We therefore recommend reporting the reference, unit, label mapping, and aggregation rule alongside every result.

Benchmarks are useful precisely because they impose a target, yet they may impose different targets. TRUE (C11) converts heterogeneous datasets to a binary grounding-consistency format. This enables common AUROC evaluation, but collapsing contradiction, absence of support, and other original labels loses distinctions that matter for diagnosis. HaluEval (C06) adds deliberately constructed errors, while RAGTruth (C12) annotates outputs from specified generators and tasks. These distributions should not be treated as interchangeable estimates of real-world error prevalence.

## RQ2: What mechanisms do evaluation methods use?

The five-family taxonomy describes information and computation, rather than mutually exclusive brands of systems. Evidence-based methods first create an object to verify, such as an atomic claim, entity relation, or question-answer pair, and then compare it with a reference or retrieved material (C07; I042; I086). Their performance depends jointly on decomposition coverage, evidence retrieval, verification, and aggregation. A claim marked unsupported because no passage was found is not necessarily false. The small source-coverage counterexample documented for C07 illustrates why evidence recall must be reported separately from the verifier’s reasoning quality.

Trained evaluators and entailment methods learn a consistency signal from supervised or synthetic pairs (C09; I073; I092). Internal-state probes use activations or attention as a proxy for risk (I090 and related studies). These approaches may be cheaper at inference than repeated API calls, but their access to model internals, training data, and transfer assumptions must be stated. AlignScore (C09), for example, has stronger full-average results on TRUE than its average after training-overlapping datasets are removed; the difference motivates leakage-aware meta-evaluation, not a simplistic causal estimate of contamination.

Self-consistency and uncertainty methods exploit disagreement among samples or model confidence without requiring a trusted external source (C08; C10; I083). Their advantage is an information regime: they can operate when retrieval is unavailable. Their limit is epistemic. A model can repeatedly produce the same false claim, and a true but unusual detail can vary across samples. Semantic entropy (C10) improves over token-level entropy in its reported QA setting, but relies on semantic equivalence decisions and specific sampling and label protocols. Thus, uncertainty is a risk ranking signal, not a proof of factual truth.

LLM-as-a-judge methods use general models to apply a rubric or verify a claim (I065; I100; I104). Their flexible natural-language interface can support multiple tasks, but prompt design, output parsing, position effects, judge identity, and access to gold evidence can alter the answer. C13 gives a concrete warning: a chain-of-thought prompt improves the reported GPT-4 segment F1 while reducing ChatGPT’s F1 in the same study. We should therefore compare judge protocols under matched examples, information access, model versions, and call budgets. Hybrid systems such as ARES (I044) combine learned judges with human calibration; they need separate claims about aggregate estimation and per-instance detection.

## RQ3: When can scores be trusted?

The first requirement is to align a metric with its intended decision. System-level correlations are relevant to comparing generators, while sample-level AUROC and risk–coverage curves address filtering. Span overlap F1 tests localization; calibration addresses probability quality. None alone measures all of these. C07 reports low overall estimation error for some generators but explicitly distinguishes that from fact-level classification. In C12, answer-level F1 is much higher than span-level F1, showing that detecting whether any error exists is easier than locating the exact text. In I044, aggregate support-rate estimates can be close to human rates even when the underlying judge has much lower per-example accuracy. A deployment claim must name the unit it acts on.

The second requirement is a defensible reference standard. Human judgments require an explicit rubric, adjudication and agreement reporting; 201 of 1908 sentences were double annotated in the SelfCheckGPT study (C08). Different papers report raw agreement, Cohen’s kappa, or correlations, which are not directly comparable. Gold labels may also be uncertain: C11’s targeted error analysis found many disputed labels among examples where several metrics all failed, a conditional sample that cannot be converted into a whole-benchmark error rate. We recommend retaining original multi-class labels and uncertain cases alongside any binary mapping.

The third requirement is transfer and resource accounting. A result obtained with gold documents, a selected Wikipedia page, or a domain-trained judge does not establish open-web retrieval or unseen-domain performance. ARES (I044) combines synthetic domain training with human corrections; its paper’s aggregate-level performance does not eliminate the domain-specific labeling cost. C10’s main QA protocol removes original task context, which changes what “knowing the answer” means. Further studies should report leave-domain, leave-generator, and multilingual tests, as well as inference calls, training costs, retrieval costs, and human annotation. Historical API prices or model snapshots should be identified as such.

## Recommendations for method selection

For summarization or RAG with an authoritative source, start by testing source faithfulness at the smallest unit needed by the application, and measure whether the evidence retriever actually finds relevant passages. For long-form open-domain answers, use claim-level evidence checking and report abstention and information quantity. When external evidence is unavailable, uncertainty and self-consistency can prioritize review but cannot certify correctness. For high-stakes decisions, the evidence standard and human escalation protocol should reflect the cost of a false negative and a false positive. In every setting, validate on examples from the target task rather than transferring a leaderboard score without its label definition and resource conditions.
